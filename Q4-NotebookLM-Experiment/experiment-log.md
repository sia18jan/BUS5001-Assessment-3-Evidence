# Q4 – NotebookLM Experiment Log

## Purpose

This experiment evaluated NotebookLM as a source-grounded generative AI tool for developing an ESG and sustainability policy briefing. The experiment focused on whether the tool could synthesise authoritative sources, generate practical organisational insights, preserve source traceability, and identify limitations or unsupported inferences.

## Notebook

Notebook title:

ESG Policy Briefing – BUS5001 Q4

## Sources Used

Three authoritative sources were added to the NotebookLM notebook:

1. United Nations – The 17 Sustainable Development Goals
2. Australian Government Climate Change Authority – official website
3. Australian Treasury – Sustainable Finance Roadmap

These sources were selected to provide both global sustainability context and Australian policy and sustainable-finance context.

## Experiment 1 – ESG Policy Briefing

NotebookLM was prompted to synthesise the selected sources into an ESG policy briefing.

The generated briefing considered:

- global sustainability goals and the UN SDGs;
- Australian climate policy and emissions targets;
- sustainable finance and climate disclosure;
- transition planning;
- circular economy and resource efficiency;
- organisational priorities and practical actions; and
- limitations and uncertainties in the available source material.

The output demonstrated that NotebookLM could combine information from multiple selected sources into a structured management-oriented briefing while retaining source references.

## Experiment 2 – Claim Audit

A second experiment critically evaluated factual claims made in the generated briefing against the selected source material.

Five claims were examined, including:

- the Climate Change Authority's 2035 emissions reduction target recommendation;
- the Sustainable Finance Roadmap;
- the United Nations 2030 Agenda and 17 SDGs;
- the Australian Carbon Credit Unit scheme review; and
- the relationship between circular economy concepts, Net Zero and selected SDGs.

Claims were classified according to the extent to which the selected sources explicitly supported them.

The audit identified both supported claims and cases where the generated briefing had introduced interpretation or conceptual synthesis beyond the explicit wording of the sources.

## Experiment 3 – Mind Map

NotebookLM's Mind Map function was used to convert the source-grounded research into a visual knowledge structure.

The central topic was:

"Global Sustainability and Australian Climate Policy Frameworks"

The mind map organised the research into four principal branches:

- Global Sustainability Goals (UN SDGs)
- Australian Climate Policy and Targets
- Sustainable Finance and Disclosure
- Circular Economy and Industrial Priorities

This provided a visual representation of the relationship between the major themes identified across the selected sources.

## Key Finding

NotebookLM was useful for rapid source-grounded synthesis and for organising multiple policy sources into a coherent briefing and visual structure. The claim-audit experiment also demonstrated why generated outputs still require human verification: some statements were reasonable conceptual interpretations but were not explicitly stated in the underlying source material.

## Governance Implication

Source citations improve traceability but do not eliminate the need for verification. For high-stakes organisational policy or compliance work, users should distinguish explicitly supported source statements from inference, synthesis and recommendation before relying on generated outputs.
