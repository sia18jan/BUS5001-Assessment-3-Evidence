# Q4 – NotebookLM Prompts

## Experiment 1 – ESG Policy Briefing

### Prompt

Using only the selected sources in this notebook, prepare a concise ESG and sustainability policy briefing for senior management.

The briefing should:

- compare the global sustainability framework with the Australian regulatory and policy context;
- identify the three most important sustainability priorities emerging from the sources;
- explain why each priority matters to an organisation;
- recommend practical organisational actions based on the source material; and
- clearly identify limitations, uncertainties or gaps in the available sources.

Do not introduce unsupported factual claims. Ground the response in the selected sources and retain source citations.

### Purpose

This prompt tested NotebookLM's ability to synthesise multiple authoritative sources into a source-grounded, management-oriented briefing.

## Experiment 2 – Factual Claim Audit

### Prompt

Audit five important factual claims from the previous ESG policy briefing strictly against the selected sources.

For each claim:

1. State the claim.
2. Identify the exact source supporting it.
3. Explain what the source actually says.
4. Classify the claim as SUPPORTED, PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, or NOT SUPPORTED.
5. Identify any inference, interpretation or extrapolation that goes beyond the source.

Do not use outside knowledge. Evaluate the claims only against the sources available in this notebook.

### Purpose

This prompt tested whether NotebookLM could be used not only for synthesis but also for source verification and identification of unsupported inference.

## Experiment 3 – Mind Map

### Topic

Global Sustainability and Australian Climate Policy Frameworks

### Source Scope

The same three selected sources were retained for the visual knowledge-mapping experiment.

### Resulting Main Themes

The generated mind map organised the material into:
